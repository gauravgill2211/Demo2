Executive summary

The employer channel is a meaningful opportunity, with a specific gap Oura is positioned to close

01

A $500M+ near-term opportunity

The employer market has the potential to be a meaningful, over $500M opportunity for Oura.

02

A gap between two extremes

The market splits between low-touch general subsidy for DIY health behaviors, which Oura already serves, and cost-of-care-centric managed services focused on the highest-cost 5 to 10% of employees, such as Transcarent.

03

A preventable-cost window

Nearly 40% of employees ("movable middle") carry preventable cost within a two-year horizon. The historical barrier has been solving this at an acceptable cost to serve.

04

Oura's right to win

Anchors on hardware and software-driven engagement: high daily engagement and health signals Oura can use to capture attention and drive activation, overcoming the cost-to-serve barrier.

05

The path forward

Builds on the consumer demand Oura already has. Employer subsidy and co-promotion accelerate adoption beyond what Oura could reach alone.

06

The core tension to solve

Growing a hardware-constrained user base through a B2B model: employer-paid PMPM must justify a 2:1 to 3:1 ROI over two years. The employee value proposition reaches the total population; the employer value proposition rests on the movable middle with in-year preventable cost.

The Market Today

Billions in spend, but no model turns risk into action

Employer health spend sits in distinct, non-overlapping pools. Each solves a different part of the problem.

Workplace Wellness
Care Navigation & Advocacy
Condition Programs
Virtual Care

Plan / Claims Navigation

Strength

Deep support for the highest-cost, most complex cases

Limitation

Claims-based triggers arrive after the cost is already locked in

Condition Programs

Strength

Structured, clinically designed pathways for a specific condition

Limitation

Needs enrollment and ongoing engagement most participants do not sustain

High-Touch Navigation

Strength

Personal and effective for the members who stay engaged

Limitation

Expensive to staff; cannot scale to the broad middle population

Wellness / Wearables

Strength

Broad reach, low cost, high enrollment

Limitation

Rarely connects a signal to a specific clinical action

Bottom line: no one combines continuous engagement with immediate activation into the right care path.
