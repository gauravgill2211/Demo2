Considerations

Most navigation is already embedded in health plan/TPA offerings; displacing it requires deep plan integration, admin capabilities and scale. Oura is more credible as a high-engagement challenger layer than a replacement.

Considerations

Own the AI layer, partner downstream: Oura can own the front door, but AI is rapidly commoditizing; establishing a right to win may require routing to — rather than competing with — Teladoc/MDLive, which raises the strategic role of Counsel.

Considerations

More hardware permission, higher clinical stakes, and the strongest brand credibility of the three: Oura must decide how far up the clinical spectrum to own, or find a partner whose value increases enough to justify the partnership.

NAVNavigation

Increasingly commoditized as national carriers and independent players (Accolade, Personify) converge on the same proactive, AI-enabled model. Oura’s edge isn’t a provider network or claims scale, so a standalone right to win here is limited.

AIAI-First Virtual Care

Oura’s engagement and signals could route meaningfully more people into AI-first triage than a partner achieves alone. But the underlying AI layer is commoditizing fast as foundation models close the gap, so this reads more as a wedge toward condition-specific care than a durable destination on its own.

CONDCondition-Specific

Condition-specific health connects Oura’s strong, large DTC opportunity with areas employers already buy. Several sub-verticals, including sleep, metabolic, women’s health and GLP-1, combine real employer spend with unmet need, while Oura’s continuous physiological signals and engagement create a differentiated, harder-to-replicate role in the health journey.
