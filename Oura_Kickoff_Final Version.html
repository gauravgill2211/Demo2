Why We Are Here

Current State, Pressure & Need

The current state, the pressure, and the need driving this engagement.

OURA
Strategic Sizing

The Current State

Oura has seen significant growth in the wearable space with the Ring, and some market activation with the membership, but will require innovation to drive a market size and potential that delivers during IPO.

The Pressure

Oura is the market owner for sensors in this space, but is facing increasing pressure in both products and member subscriptions, with competitors getting creative to gain market share.

The Need

Oura needs to understand the full suite of market dollars that could be accessible, and how new and advanced business models may drive higher revenue. These pools of dollars will shape near and long-term decisions.

Market Sizing Workstream

Market Categories & Data Sources

How we will approach data for Market Sizing: the spending categories in scope and where each will come from.

OURA
Strategic Sizing

Consumer Wellness Economy

GWI sector

Broad wellness economy spend spanning activity, nutrition, mental wellness, and personal care, including Oura's own Personal Consumer Products and Wellness Subscriptions.

  • Personal Consumer Products
  • Wellness Subscriptions
  • Physical Activity (incl. Fitness Technology, Smart Wearables)
  • Personal Care & Beauty
  • Healthy Eating, Nutrition, & Weight Loss (incl. Vitamins & Supplements)
  • Wellness Tourism
  • Public Health, Prevention, & Personalized Medicine (incl. Diagnostics, Personalized Health)
  • Wellness Real Estate
  • Mental Wellness (incl. Meditation & Mindfulness, Sleep)
  • Traditional & Complementary Medicine
  • Workplace Wellness

Spas and Thermal/Mineral Springs are also captured in this dataset but not called out individually here, given limited near-term relevance to Oura.

Insurance Related Spending

By LOB

Health insurance premiums paid by consumers or subsidized by employers, across individual and employer-sponsored plans, plus supplemental/voluntary insurance products.

  • Medicare (MA + Part D)
  • Group (fully-insured)
  • Medicaid (managed care)
  • Individual (ACA)
  • Supplemental Insurance

Medical Out-of-Pocket (OOP)

Cost-share

Direct medical costs consumers pay in addition to premiums, i.e. deductibles, copays, and coinsurance.

  • Deductibles
  • Cost Sharing (Copays & Coinsurance)

OOP Non-Covered Spend

Self-pay

Out-of-pocket spend not covered by insurance, including OTC Rx and GLP-1 costs paid directly by consumers.

  • GLP-1
  • OTC Rx

Data Request List

Information requested from Oura to support the market sizing analysis, mapped to each relevant category below.

OURA
Strategic Sizing
Category Legend Insurance Pharmacy (GLP-1) Wearables Wellness Subscriptions
Data Requested Purpose Applies To
Membership volume (e.g., total active subscribers, new signups per period, cancellations and returning members), and attrition and retention rate (e.g., monthly or annual attrition %, by tenure cohort), trailing 3-year trend Establishes Oura's current base and trajectory, the benchmark used across all sizing categories and the input for a realistic 5-year projection
Age, gender, and income band of current members Enables alignment of Oura's member base against public market benchmarks segmented the same way
Revenue split by line of business (device sales vs. subscription fees) Allows current revenue to be allocated directly across the Wellness Subscriptions and Wearables categories
Employer, insurer, health-plan, HSA/FSA, or wellness-incentive channels currently subsidizing ring or membership enrollment, and associated volume Establishes the premium-linked revenue Oura already captures today across all subsidy channels, and prevents double-counting this revenue within Wearables
Current membership volume self-reporting GLP-1 or other prescription medication use (e.g., via onboarding survey responses, in-app health profile fields, or customer support inquiries) The primary input for sizing this category, capturing Oura's overlap with the fastest-growing pharmacy-driven segment
Marketing or acquisition data on GLP-1-related campaigns and associated conversion rates (e.g., paid ads or landing pages referencing GLP-1/weight-loss tracking, and signups attributed to them) Informs whether GLP-1-driven growth is expected to continue over the next five years or reflects a one-time increase
Ring hardware sales volume, revenue, and replacement/repeat-purchase rate, by generation or model (e.g., warranty replacement due to loss or damage vs. voluntary upgrade to a newer generation) Establishes the revenue baseline for this category and distinguishes one-time purchases from recurring revenue, directly impacting the 5-year growth assumption
Existence and structure of any device protection plan, extended warranty, or accessory program (e.g., price point, coverage terms, opt-in rate at time of purchase) Identifies whether a new recurring-revenue sub-line should be added within Wearables for the 5-year projection
Current membership subscription price by plan/tier (e.g., monthly vs. annual billing, ring-bundled vs. standalone membership, any regional or promotional pricing), and the date and amount of any price changes over the trailing 3 years Needed to reconstruct actual historical subscription revenue by pairing price at each point in time with subscriber volume, rather than assuming a flat price held constant over the 3-year trend
Analysis of primary cancellation drivers (price, engagement drop-off, feature gaps) Supports a realistic retention assumption within the 5-year projection
