Employer economics determine what they actually buy

The more health risk an employer carries, the more it values measurable health-cost impact relative to a standalone wellness experience.

Employer archetype
Who carries claims risk?
What the employer primarily optimizes for
Risk Implications
Fully insurediKFF definitionAn insurance arrangement in which the employer contracts with a health plan that assumes financial responsibility for the costs of enrollees' medical claims.
Health plan / insurer
Premium predictability, employee experience, low admin burden, attraction and retention
Limited standalone growth potential for Oura. Fully insured employers carry none of the claims risk themselves. They pay a fixed premium and the insurer absorbs whatever claims come in that year, good or bad. Because there is no risk on their side to manage, these employers are not shopping for cost-control tools. They are buying predictability and a plan their employees are happy with, which is a reasonable match for what Oura offers. The real constraint is who holds the pen. Since the insurer owns the risk, the insurer typically owns the plan design too, so the employer is not the decision-maker.
Level-fundediKFF definitionAn insurance arrangement in which the employer makes a set payment each month to an insurer or third-party administrator which funds a reserve account for claims, administrative costs, and premiums for stop-loss coverage. When claims are lower than expected, surplus claims payments may be refunded at the end of the contract.
Employer retains limited risk; stop-loss transfers substantial downside
Spend predictability plus selected claims reduction and low operational burden
A small, cushioned step toward self-funding rather than full risk-taking. The employer takes on a modest slice of risk, more than fully insured but far less than true self-funding, and buys stop-loss on top so a bad claims year still does not hurt them. If claims come in low, the employer can get money back at year end. If claims run high, stop-loss covers most of the difference. Because the risk is small and the stop-loss comes bundled in from the same insurer or third-party administrator, adoption is likely determined by that carrier's bundle rather than by independent employer selection.
37% of covered workers in firms with 10 to 199 workers are covered by a level-funded plan.
Self-funded / ASOiKFF definitionAn insurance arrangement in which the employer assumes direct financial responsibility for the costs of enrollees' medical claims. Employers sponsoring self-funded plans typically contract with a third-party administrator or insurer to provide administrative services. In some cases, the employer may buy stop-loss coverage from an insurer to protect against very large claims.
Employer directly funds claims; plan or TPA administers
Total medical cost trend, quality, productivity, access, employee experience
The strongest direct target for Oura, and a durable one given a stable share over the past decade. Stop-loss is a big part of why employers are comfortable taking on this risk in the first place. It caps their worst-case exposure, so most of the appeal of self-funding, keeping the savings when claims run low and controlling plan design, comes without the full downside.
67% of covered U.S. workers are in self-funded plans, rising to 80% among workers at firms with 200 or more employees.
