Strategic Thesis

Oura can redefine the employee health front door

Prove a narrow device-led savings pool now to credibly move into a device-agnostic clinical front door, and build capabilities toward a consumer-directed care play.

Offering
Turn trusted wearable engagement into lower-cost action for known care gaps, before members become high-cost claimants.
A device-agnostic, all-employee offering: use continuous, consented health data to make clinical navigation predictive and personal, not episodic and administrative.
Pair Oura’s consumer relationship with clinical access to become the care companion employees choose with employer-funded dollars.
Strategic bet
High-touch care management focuses on the sickest members; Oura can create measurable savings in the much larger, clinically actionable middle.
The employee health “front door” is shifting from a generic benefits portal to a patient-consented, clinically integrated layer that anticipates need and gets people to action.
As health dollars become more portable, employees will increasingly choose the care experience that best combines convenience, trust, and clinical access.
Value to Oura
Targets ~1,570 members per 10,000 employees with hypertension or sleep-apnea care gaps
Reaches ~70% of a typical employer population across six priority conditions, steering people to the right next step and site of care
Creates higher-value revenue by turning Oura’s trusted consumer relationship into telehealth, clinical-care, and pharmacy decisions, not just ring and membership sales
Strategic competitive positioning
Complement and activation wedge: re-engages members who never enroll or fall out of existing programs.
Reference points: Omada, Virta, Vida, PBM adherence programs, plan outreach.
Cross-category front-door play: competes for the engagement, triage, and low-acuity care-management spend now distributed across:
  • Digital behavior change: Noom, Omada, Vida
  • Virtual care: Teladoc, MDLive, virtual primary care
  • Navigation: Accolade, Personify, Included Health, Transcarent
Consumer care relationship: competes in the emerging market for self-directed health spend.
Reference points: cash-pay telehealth, concierge primary care, and consumer clinical platforms. Counsel could enable the clinical layer.
Capabilities required
Oura signals and Advisor; employer/plan cohort ingestion; condition-specific activation journeys; low-cost digital escalation; outcome reporting.
Patient-consented interoperability; device-agnostic data layer; Oura-enabled personalization when a ring is present; clinical AI triage; telehealth/nurse/PCP escalation; benefit and provider routing; closed-loop outcome data.
Consumer enrollment and payment; integrated clinical-care package; longitudinal health profile; agentic follow-up; stipend/ICHRA experience.
Source of willingness to pay

Employer stipend or hardware subsidy, plus consumer subscription and clinical-care spend.

How the Model Works

Business Case Logic & Assumptions

Every mechanic behind the interactive slides, and an honest read on what’s solid versus still directional.

Solid
Core Formula
Population × Engagement% × Adherence% × Value per Success = Employer Value. Employer Value ÷ 3 = Oura Fee Ceiling, assuming a 3:1 employer ROI requirement.
Solid
Segment Structure
1A: treated, falling off. 1B: known, not treated. 1C: unaware, no care. 1A+1B bundle as the device-led wedge; 1C layers in the unaware population for the full-portfolio years.
Directional
Engagement & Adherence
Ramps 30→40% (1A), 25→35% (1B), 20→30% (1C) by year; adherence flat at 30/25/15%. Benchmarked against Omada (<5% all-time enrollment) and Vitality/Apple Watch (20–40% over years). Sits at the optimistic end, not conservative.
Solid
Pricing Model (PEPM)
Same dollar pool, two ways to bill: Full population (thin PEPM, ~$3–4) or Engaged only (thick PEPM, ~$30+). A pricing-philosophy choice, not a revenue lever. Now synced live with the ship-to-population toggle on the device-economics slide.
Directional
Employer Count / Book Size
200 → 750 → 1,000 employers by year. Benchmarked down from an initial 500, against Levanto’s real “10 clients, Year 1” comparable for a brand-new employer benefits program.
Directional
Hardware Economics
(COGS − Copay × Collection Rate) × Rings Shipped. Collection rate models “pay it off with usage”: engaged members reliably complete payment, others are harder to collect from. One-time cost, judged against multi-year retention, not just Year 1.
Directional
Consumer Membership & Partner Share
$6.99/mo membership layered on top, applied only to engaged/successful members. Partner revenue share (default 35%) applies only to Prediabetes and Behavioral Health, the two conditions needing a real clinical delivery partner. Disabled in Year 1 (HTN+OSA only, no partner needed).
Placeholder
Not Yet Sourced
Counsel / consumer-directed care (“2”): no population or spend data exists yet; see the illustrative-only mechanism test. Market 1 TAM: anchored to this bottoms-up model, not an inferred competitor PMPM.
Solid: traces to a published study or a stated formula Directional: reasonable assumption, benchmarked but not proven Placeholder: no real data yet, mechanism only
